Equity Markets in FY27: What Should Long-Term Investors Watch?

Indian equities enter FY27 with a familiar tension: strong structural growth potential on one side, and elevated expectations, global uncertainty and valuation risk on the other.

August 2026 • 6–8 min read • Aayushman Financial Services

Every financial year brings a new market narrative. The challenge is to separate the narrative from the variables that can actually influence long-term wealth creation.

As FY27 progresses, the useful approach is not to predict a single index target. It is to monitor earnings, liquidity, inflation, interest rates, oil prices, currency movements and the behaviour of domestic and foreign investors.

The starting point: earnings matter

Over long periods, equity returns are closely connected to the ability of businesses to grow earnings and cash flows. A market can move ahead of fundamentals for a while, but expectations eventually have to be supported by business performance.

Domestic flows remain important

Recent July 2026 data reported by Reuters showed equity mutual-fund inflows remained positive, although total inflows declined month-on-month. SIP contributions stayed close to their record level, while foreign investors returned to buying Indian equities in July.

Domestic flows can provide a different source of demand from global portfolio flows. This does not remove market risk, but it can influence resilience during global volatility.

Valuation is the second half of the equation

A high-quality company can still be a poor investment if the purchase price leaves no room for disappointment. Instead of asking whether the market is simply cheap or expensive, investors can compare valuations with expected earnings growth, interest rates and liquidity.

The market does not reward being optimistic. It rewards being right about future cash flows at the price you paid.

Oil and geopolitics matter

India remains sensitive to crude-oil prices because of its import dependence. In August 2026, elevated crude prices and Middle East tensions have been weighing on sentiment. Reuters reported crude around the $88–$90 range during the week while Indian equities faced pressure from higher oil prices.

Mid- and small-caps need discipline

Recent flows have shown strong investor interest in smaller companies. That enthusiasm can be healthy when supported by earnings, but smaller companies can also experience larger drawdowns and greater liquidity risk.

Allocation should therefore be based on portfolio objectives rather than popularity.

What to watch through FY27

  • Corporate earnings: breadth, margins and earnings revisions.
  • Inflation and rates: their impact on borrowing costs and valuations.
  • Crude oil: a key input into India's external balance and inflation.
  • Foreign flows: useful as a sentiment indicator, not a trading signal.
  • Domestic liquidity: SIP and mutual-fund flows can influence resilience.
  • Valuations: whether expected growth is already priced in.
Our perspective:

FY27 should be approached as a portfolio-management exercise, not a one-year market bet. Maintain diversification, invest according to goals and use volatility to review risk rather than abandon the plan.

The bottom line

There is no single FY27 trade that can substitute for a sound investment process. A disciplined investor can participate in India's structural growth while accepting that markets will periodically correct, rotate and surprise.
